\chapter{Neural Calibration as an Inverse Problem}
\label{ch:cann}

The previous chapter used a neural network as a forward Heston pricer. This chapter uses the same idea in the opposite direction: given a finite implied-volatility surface, infer the Heston parameter vector
\[
\theta=(\rho,\kappa,\gamma,\bar v,v_0).
\]
Here \(\rho\) is correlation, \(\kappa\) is mean reversion, \(\gamma\) is volatility of variance, \(\bar v\) is long-run variance, and \(v_0\) is the initial variance. The chapter follows the neural calibration idea of Liu et al. \cite{liu}: a trained ANN pricer is frozen and used as a fast evaluator inside a calibration loop.

The purpose is not to introduce a new calibration algorithm. The contribution in this thesis is the integration of this calibration step into the ANN--PINN workflow and the diagnostic reading of its output. For that reason, this chapter keeps only the setup needed to interpret the benchmark in Chapter~\ref{ch:performance_benchmarks}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Calibration Setup}

The ANN surrogate from Chapter~\ref{ch:ann_surrogate} approximates the forward map
\[
f_{NN}:(\rho,\kappa,\gamma,\bar v,v_0,m,\tau,r)\mapsto \sigma_{\mathrm{IV}},
\]
where \(m=S_0/K\), \(\tau=T-t\), \(r\) is the risk-free rate, and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{IV}}\) is the implied volatility. In calibration, the quote descriptors are fixed and only \(\theta\) is varied. For each candidate \(\theta\), the network is evaluated on the quote set
\[
\mathcal{Q}=\{(m_j,\tau_j,r_j,\sigma^{\mathrm{mkt}}_j,w_j)\}_{j=1}^{N_q},
\]
where \(w_j\geq0\) is an optional quote weight. The selected benchmark uses \(N_q=35\) quotes on a complete \(5\times7\) moneyness--maturity grid with constant \(r=0.03\); the exact nodes are reported in Section~\ref{sec:cann_performance}.

For quote \(j\), the predicted implied volatility and residual are
\[
\hat\sigma_j(\theta)=f_{NN}(\rho,\kappa,\gamma,\bar v,v_0,m_j,\tau_j,r_j),
\qquad
e_j(\theta)=\hat\sigma_j(\theta)-\sigma^{\mathrm{mkt}}_j.
\]
Calibration is therefore performed in implied-volatility space, matching the ANN training target and the market convention used for volatility smiles.

The objective minimized in the calibration loop is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cann_objective}
J(\theta)
=
\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{N_q} w_j}
\sum_{j=1}^{N_q} w_j e_j(\theta)^2
+\lambda R(\theta),
\end{equation}
where \(R(\theta)\) is an optional regularization penalty. The calibrated vector is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cann_argmin}
\theta^\star
=
\arg\min_{\theta\in\mathcal{B}}J(\theta),
\end{equation}
with \(\mathcal{B}\) the admissible Heston parameter box. In this thesis, the bounds are aligned with the ANN training domain: \(\rho\in[-0.9,0]\), \(\kappa\in[0,3]\), \(\gamma\in[0.01,0.8]\), \(\bar v\in[0.01,0.5]\), and \(v_0\in[0.05,0.5]\). This restriction keeps calibration inside the intended interpolation regime of the forward surrogate.

\section{Search And Stability Checks}

The inverse residual surface is not assumed to be convex, so the reference calibration uses Differential Evolution followed by local polishing. This is a pragmatic choice: the method is robust to initialization, does not require gradients of the neural objective, and can evaluate a population of candidates through batched ANN inference.

Regularization is used only as a stability check. The benchmark compares the no-regularization objective with a small \(L^2\) penalty; the fit difference is negligible, so the no-regularization run is retained as the reference. The detailed numbers are reported in Section~\ref{sec:cann_performance}.

The selected solution is assessed at two levels. First, quote-level residuals check whether the implied-volatility surface is reproduced. Second, parameter diagnostics check whether the selected optimum is locally stable. Around a low-residual optimum, the residual data term can be read through the Gauss--Newton approximation
\[
H(\theta^\star)\approx 2J_e(\theta^\star)^\top WJ_e(\theta^\star),
\]
where \(J_e(\theta^\star)\) is the Jacobian of quote residuals with respect to \(\theta\), and \(W\) is the diagonal matrix of quote weights. Large curvature indicates directions where the quote surface reacts strongly to parameter changes; small curvature indicates flatter, less identified directions.

This reading is deliberately cautious. A small residual confirms that the calibrated vector matches the quote grid, but it does not prove global uniqueness of the Heston inverse problem. The benchmark in Chapter~\ref{ch:performance_benchmarks} therefore reports both fit quality and parameter-level diagnostics.

\section{Interface With The PINN Stage}

The calibrated vector becomes the market state used by the downstream PINN analysis. The structural parameters \((\rho^\star,\kappa^\star,\gamma^\star,\bar v^\star)\) define the Heston operator, while \(v_0^\star\) is inserted as the variance-state coordinate when evaluating the trained pricing model. This connects the ANN pricer, the calibration loop, and the PINN-based pricing and Greek computation into a single pipeline.
